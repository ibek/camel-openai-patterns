Risk Scoring Rubric:

1. CRITICAL (Score 90-100) - SECURITY INCIDENT:
   - Triggers:
     A) Technical: SQL injection, XSS, remote code execution.
     B) Social Engineering: Requests to bypass MFA/2FA, password resets via unverified channels (Gmail/SMS), or impersonation of executives ("I am the CEO").
     C) Urgency: Artificial time pressure ("Payroll in 1 hour", "Do it now").
   - Action: Immediate lock of account and page SecOps.

2. HIGH (Score 70-89) - LEGAL/COMMERCIAL:
   - Triggers: GDPR/CCPA requests, lawsuits, SLA breaches with financial threats.
   - Action: Route to Legal or VP of Success.

3. MEDIUM (Score 50-69) - CHURN RISK:
   - Triggers: "Cancel my account", "Too expensive", swearing/hostility.
   - Action: Route to Retention Specialist.

4. LOW (Score 0-49) - STANDARD:
   - Triggers: Bugs, "How to", Feature requests.

Interaction Style Guide:

1. PROFESSIONAL:
   - Use this for "White Hat" hackers, auditors, or legal notices.
   - Indicators: Technical vocabulary ("dependency", "CVE", "injection"), objective statement of facts, absence of exclamation marks or emotional words.
   - Context: The user is NOT asking for help; they are providing information.

2. FRUSTRATED:
   - Use this when the user is reacting to a service failure.
   - Indicators: Complaining about bugs, downtime, or bad UI.
   - Key Difference: The user wants the product to work as intended; they do not ask to bypass security.

3. MANIPULATIVE_URGENT:
   - Use this when the user tries to force an action by creating artificial consequences.
   - Indicators: "Do this now or else", name-dropping executives, requesting bypass of standard procedure ("I can't use the portal"), or channel switching ("Email my Gmail").
   - Key Difference: The user is NOT reporting a bug; they are demanding a policy violation.

4. HOSTILE:
   - Use this for personal attacks, profanity, or threats of violence.
